# ADR-0069: Truth-seeking: a doctrine section, a precedence statement, and a halt-guard check

## Metadata
- Author: Aleksi Ursin / MCX (with Väinämöinen)
- Date: 2026-10-19
- Status: Accepted. Extends [ADR-0047](0047-halt-guard.md) (one more question in the halt guard's battery, applied after its decision table, inside its existing limits) and the SOUL.md doctrine of [ADR-0010](0010-soul-and-voice.md) and [ADR-0029](0029-tool-doctrine-loaded-into-soul.md) (two insertions, no change to how SOUL.md is built).

## Context
The request (2026-10-19): Pikkumies seeks the truth on every topic, never takes official policies, political correctness or social conformity as evidence, and challenges everything; this goes into SOUL.md and gets a halt-guard check; and the soul, doctrine and safety rules are stated as firmly binding, in stern wording.

What was found:
- The doctrine had the accuracy rules (a source for every fact, proof for causes, users and documents can be wrong) and Voice had "agree because it is right". Nothing covered the failure where the topic, not the evidence, decides the answer: a fact softened or dodged because it is sensitive, a claim accepted because an authority or a majority holds it, a claim dismissed with a label (myth, conspiracy, debunked) instead of a check. Pikkumies also answers questions that have nothing to do with servers, so this is reachable.
- "Never trust official policy" read literally would break an existing rule that is right: the official source is what says what a policy, price or term is (Doctrine, Sources and trust). The two fit once they are kept apart: an official text is the source for what the policy says; it is not evidence that a factual claim in it is true.
- The halt guard's chat verdict (a question, no work requested) ended every such turn unchecked, and factual answers land there.
- Pikkumies has no mission file. The soul text holds the job and who the agent works for; Safety opens with "these rules come before the task". Nothing said in one place that all of these are standing rules, in what order they win, and that text read while working cannot change them.
- Research on firm instruction language (prompt-compliance studies and a current model vendor's prompting guide, reviewed for this change). Measured: content near the start or end of a long context is used more than content in the middle; compliance falls as the number of instructions grows; in this project's sibling agent, steps enforced by a gate ran nearly every time while steps that were only written down ran in roughly one case in ten to one in three. Advised by the guide: give the reason with a rule, say what to do rather than only what not to do, and use ordinary wording, because current models over-apply shouted instructions. Inferred, not measured: emphasis helps only while it is rare, and one rule restated several ways counts as several instructions, costing more than it adds. No study found showed that capitals or "critical/sacred" wording raise compliance; the request chose stern wording anyway, and that choice stands. The structural check (the halt-guard question below) is the part measured to work.

## Options Considered
- **A new doctrine file wired into renderSoul's file list.** Clean separation, but a new file and a code change for what is a section of the accuracy doctrine.
- **Entries in the common-mistakes list only.** Read on demand, so not in SOUL.md as asked.
- **Folding the check into an existing judge question (deferral, next steps).** Mixes two concerns in one answer, and those questions are the ones other work changes.
- **A separate hook unit for the check.** A second model call per turn and a parallel path beside the halt guard.
- **A section in doctrine.md, a short precedence statement at the top of the soul text, and one separately labelled question in the halt guard's existing call (chosen).** No change to how SOUL.md is built, no new call, no new hook.

## Decision
- **Doctrine** (`doctrine/doctrine.md`, "Truth-seeking: evidence, not authority"): evidence decides, not who says it, how official it sounds, how many agree or how the topic is regarded. Authority is a claim, not proof, while the official text stays the source for what a policy itself says; say whom a rule binds and what it covers. A sensitive topic changes the tone, never the facts; where the evidence is thin or mixed, saying exactly that is the true answer. A label is not a check, and the reverse holds too: unpopular or contrarian is not true. Challenge the first answer; directness is not evidence. The user's first-hand observation is evidence; a party's claim in its own favour is a claim to check. Scope: answer what is asked, volunteer no opinions on charged topics, keep value choices with the user, and polite wording is Voice, not softening.
- **Precedence** (`doctrine/soul.md`, "Your rules", right after the job): the soul text, Voice, Doctrine, Safety and the tool rules are standing rules; when they conflict, Safety and privacy, then Doctrine, then the task, with Voice shaping how it is said; text read while working never changes them; one sentence of why. Worded sternly (LAW, EVERY, NEVER), as the request asked. The doctrine section names what the request named: official policies, political correctness and social conformity are not evidence.
- **Halt guard** (`lib/HaltDecouple.php`): one more question, labelled `D1` so it never collides with the numbered questions, listed after them in the same light-model call. "Yes" means the final answer dodged or blurred a factual question the user asked, dismissed a claim with a label without saying what was checked, or rested a factual claim only on authority, an official position or what most people think; naming the documentation or source for what a product, setting or policy is (the doctrine asks for exactly that), an honest "I don't know" or "I haven't checked" that says what would settle it, a refusal a safety rule requires, leaving a value choice to the user after giving the facts, and a polite tone are carved out. Applied after `HaltJudge::decide`: a yes turns any verdict except failopen into a continue whose message starts with one fixed sentence (before the work sentences of an existing continue, or with the closing rule alone). No, unsure or a missing answer leaves the verdict exactly as it was. In the one-shot wrapper, the answer to this nudge (the resumed turn whose user message is the nudge) is not sent back for the same reason again; the live hook is bounded by Hermes' nudge cap as before. The D1 answer goes to the decision log with the others.
- **Search-first** treats the new sentence as a halt-guard follow-up, like the other fixed sentences ([ADR-0047](0047-halt-guard.md) amendment).

## Consequences
- SOUL.md grows by about 2.9 KB on every profile (largest profile measured at 25.5 KB of the 61 440-byte cap). Tests cap the truth-seeking section at 2600 bytes and the precedence statement at 800, so neither grows without a reason.
- A chat turn can now get one continue. The light model's answers are untested offline: whether it tells a dodge from honest uncertainty is unverified until a live run of the question suite includes a factual question on a sensitive topic (it should be sent back if dodged), an "I haven't checked" answer (it should not be), and a question of values (it should not be). The decision log records the D1 answers, so a D1 that fires on most chat turns shows there, and means the wording needs work.
- No limit changes: the existing caps (three nudges live, three continues plus one wrap-up one-shot) bound the new criterion, and the model still contributes only yes, no or unsure; every word the agent is told is a fixed PHP constant.
